Why the difficulty is meaningful

Observable, learnable decisions

  • Manuals specify geometry, OPA path, and instrument ranges.
  • The camera reports focal spots; the detector and delay stage reveal timing.
  • Every action costs simulated lab time.

Physics in the loop

  • Ray tracing, clipping on optics and mounts, lens focusing.
  • Polarisation, detector wavelength limits, noise, and stage backlash.
  • Seeded labs make reruns reproducible.
The task is difficult because the agent must infer a procedure and correct errors while the clock runs. We do not claim the simulator is a full wave-optics model or that every parameter was independently validated against hardware.

What the current score does—and misses

The score multiplies beam throughput, spatial overlap, and a narrow temporal overlap term. Cost and time apply as penalties.

A run can align the spots well and still score zero when the pulses miss by many picoseconds. A more graded progress signal is a future design option, not a result in this deck.

The critique correctly identifies a weakness for training: all non-successful runs can look alike. We have not changed the scoring code, so this slide states the limitation instead of presenting a proposed rubric as implemented.
